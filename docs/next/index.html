Step 6 of 6

Limits and the next step

This is a planning aid. It supports county emergency plans and does not replace them. Here is what it won't do, what still needs a person, and how it grows.

What it will never do

  • ✕

    Hold, ask for, or link any record about a person. No registry, no tracking, no names or addresses. Only counts per area, with small counts hidden by the owner.

  • ✕

    Replace county or State emergency plans, county shelter decisions, or alerting systems. Roles, decision points and messages are proposals for counties and State ESF-6 to confirm.

  • ✕

    Say an area is safe, a site is open, or a shelter has room. A listed site is not an open site; only a current official source can say that.

  • ✕

    Connect to or run inside DHS systems. It is a static site and printable briefs built from public data.

How it grows

Each stage is worth doing even if the next never comes.

  1. 1

    Now

    Briefs, maps and labels from public data. Nothing new required.

    Needs: nothing new

  2. 2

    Next season

    Each county posts one dated shelter sheet a season. Partners post pledge sheets.

    Needs: cooperation

  3. 3

    Later

    Shared words at a stable address. Partners with client records answer with counts per area.

    Needs: an agreement

  4. 4

    A policy decision for DHS

    Whether residents may volunteer their own needs. We only name the decision.

    Needs: a DHS or legislative decision

How AI was used

AI agents did the building, researching and drafting. Every number comes from plain code that anyone can re-run. No person outside the team has reviewed the output yet.

What a person still has to check (8)
  • Every role, decision point and trigger, by the county emergency management agency and State ESF-6. (not yet done)
  • Resident message drafts and any translations, by native speakers and trusted community messengers, for accuracy, tone and trauma-informed wording. (not yet done)
  • Planning factors marked team_assumption (tsunami and flood shelter rates, the low hurricane band) and whether 19% is still the State's design rate. (not yet done)
  • The wildfire zone rule, against real county evacuation areas. (not yet done)
  • The back-test reading: whether the agreed margin and the chosen past events are fair. (not yet done)
  • The mapping of each county's shelter words to the shared site types. (not yet done)
  • Level-0 labels: that the named office is the right one to ask. (not yet done)
  • The goal-3 baseline and target, which are team assumptions. (not yet done)

Our ask

One county. One timed tabletop.

Same planners, same scenario: once the way it's done today, then once with the brief. That measures DHS goal 3, the time it takes to produce a coordinated plan.

Our questions for DHS (5)
  • How long does it take today to put together a needs-and-resources plan for one county and one hazard, and would one county join a timed tabletop to measure it before and after?
  • Would each county share one shelter sheet per season (site type, planned capacity, access, power, pets, last confirmed), published or shared with ESF-6 partners only?
  • Who should own the shared words: HIEMA and DHS as an attachment to the SESF #6 Annex at its next review, the Chief Data Officer as a State data standard, or both?
  • For goal 2, is 'the published shelter count falls inside the model's low-to-high range' an acceptable margin, and which past events should count?
  • Which partners who hold people-level records (utilities, dialysis and home-health providers through the health coalition, Med-QUEST plans) would share counts per area, and under what agreement?
