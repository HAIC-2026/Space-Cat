Checked against past events

DHS asked for estimates checked against past disasters. We ran the same shelter formula on the area each past event affected and put it next to the number of people who actually went to shelters.

“Generate needs estimates for each hazard across the CMIST categories. County, DHS, and partner organizations to validate as reasonable [target: e.g., within an agreed margin against past events].”

DHS challenge brief, p. 7

Wildfire and tsunami

Real turnouts landed in the range or above it: Maui 2023, about 2,100 people; the 2025 tsunami warning, about 1,500 on Hawaiʻi Island and 1,800 on Kauaʻi.

Plan with the high end of the range.

Hurricane

The published planning rate describes a direct hit. Every storm since ʻIniki (1992) was a near miss: Lane brought 1,114 people to Oʻahu shelters against a planning case of 190,000.

Size for the direct hit; staff for what is forecast.

Rates come from published studies and were never tuned to these events. Where the formula is far off, the table says so.
