The question we had to answer

DHS says the information exists but isn't connected. So before building anything, we had to decide what to build first.

The question

Given that the facts exist but are not connected, what should DHS and its partners build first?

Why aren't the facts connected?

We opened the real data sources and checked. The facts are real, but they carry no date and no shared words.

Six root causes

  1. 1

    No shared words

    Each county, and each shared system, names shelters and places its own way, so lists cannot be lined up without a person reading them.

    Show the evidence (2)
    • In FEMA's ESF#6 Shelter System, the county field on Hawaiʻi's 729 records has 12 distinct values: the four counties are spelled 9 ways (for example "HONOLULU" 377 and "Honolulu" 33, "Kauai" with a trailing space), plus three values that are not Hawaiʻi counties or are a town. source, checked 2026-11-14
    • Kauaʻi calls every site a "refuge area" ("an evacuation option of last resort"); Hawaiʻi County's layer uses Pre-Impact and Post-Impact types with no published definition; Maui's 2026 releases use a Temporary Evacuation Point and a shelter; HI-EMA has no page that defines "hurricane refuge area". source, checked 2026-11-14
  2. 2

    No date anyone checked it

    Sources say when a file last changed, not when someone last confirmed it was true, so a planner cannot tell a current fact from a stale one without calling.

    Show the evidence (2)
    • Neither State catalog fills the two fields that say how current a dataset is: `temporal` and `accrualPeriodicity` are never present on any of the 963 datasets at opendata.hawaii.gov or the 425 at the Statewide GIS portal. source, checked 2026-11-14
    • Kauaʻi's public county shelter layer (service named "Shelters_Testing_Copy", last edited 2026-09-23) carries CAPACITY values whose attributes mostly date from August 2015, with no unit, basis or date. FEMA's shelter system has no per-record "last verified" field at all. source, checked 2026-11-14
  3. 3

    Rebuilt every event, never kept

    Lists that exist are kept private or announced only when sites open, so the same picture is put together by hand again for each storm.

    Show the evidence (2)
    • The Legislature said in 2025 that "at present no county offers a comprehensive electronically accessible list on a government-run website that provides crucial information such as the category of hurricane the facility is able to sustain, capacity limits, accommodations for access and functional needs individuals, or the ability to shelter pets", and urged each county to publish one (House Resolution 159 H.D. 1). source, checked 2026-11-14
    • Maui County publishes no standing list: in 2019 MEMA said it "stopped publishing a list because not all shelters are opened for all emergency events" and keeps a "non-published list of predesignated shelters". Honolulu's tsunami after-action report says "DEM maintains a database of potential sites" that is not public. source, checked 2026-11-14
  4. 4

    Shared systems with empty boxes

    Where a shared system already exists, the facts planners need most are blank, because nobody's job is to fill and confirm them.

    Show the evidence (2)
    • Of FEMA's 729 Hawaiʻi shelter records, `generator_onsite` is YES on 8, NO on 103, UNK on 64 and blank or null on 554; `wheelchair_accessible` is YES on 58 and blank or null on 578. No Hawaiʻi record says pets are allowed. source, checked 2026-11-14
    • Hawaiʻi County's own shelter layer (77 sites, last edited 2026-09-27) has an `ADA` field that is blank or N/A for 69 sites and a `Power` field blank for 64. source, checked 2026-11-14
  5. 5

    Records or nothing

    The people-level facts sit in client and customer records that cannot be shared, and there is no agreed habit of sharing counts per area instead.

    Show the evidence (2)
    • Hawaiian Electric and KIUC keep medical-needs and life-support customer lists, but neither publishes a count of enrolled accounts by area (unknown). HHS emPOWER shows the other way: counts of electricity-dependent Medicare beneficiaries per ZIP, with 1 to 10 shown as 11. source, checked 2026-11-14
    • The State's Data Classification Guidelines (CDO-004) say "When sharing data between different agencies within the State, a data sharing agreement is required unless it is public data", and no published statewide data-sharing agreement template was found. source, checked 2026-11-14
  6. 6

    Nobody holds the whole picture

    The State has no current roll-up of shelters or partner resources, and partners are reached by phone, so gaps show up only when someone calls.

    Show the evidence (2)
    • No state agency publishes a current statewide shelter or refuge list: the State's only open-data shelter table says "Date Updated: June 18, 2012", and the Statewide GIS Program has no shelter layer. Civil Beat (August 2026, a secondary source) reports HIEMA "was unable to provide a breakdown of island storm shelters and their level of fortitude". source, checked 2026-11-14
    • Partner pledges (cots, meals, staff), paratransit capacity, interpreters, and donations are all at level 0: none of the four county paratransit pages gives a fleet size, and the State has "no visibility" into donations. source, checked 2026-11-14

What a good answer has to do

DHS set these rules in the challenge brief.

  1. 1

    Work alongside what exists

    “The plan must work alongside existing state and county emergency plans and partner roles, not replace them.”

    DHS challenge brief, p. 5
  2. 2

    Keep going after the challenge

    “Feasibility: the framework can operate within existing IT infrastructure and be maintained after the challenge.”

    DHS challenge brief, p. 7
  3. 3

    Never hold records about people

    “Out of scope: use of personally identifiable client data; individual-level registries or tracking”

    DHS challenge brief, p. 6
  4. 4

    Work when things go wrong

    “DHS staff and partners must be able to realistically use the framework for planning and during high-stress, low-connectivity conditions.”

    DHS challenge brief, p. 5
